Which GCSE subjects can you resit in November 2026?

In England, the November GCSE examination series is primarily for GCSE English Language and GCSE Mathematics. These are the two subjects most commonly associated with November resits, particularly for students who need to improve a previous result.

This does not mean every GCSE subject is available in November. Subjects such as Biology, Chemistry, Physics, History and Geography are generally examined in the summer series rather than the standard November GCSE resit series.

Arrangements in Wales and Northern Ireland can differ, including Welsh-language qualifications and particular legacy resit specifications. Students should check which qualifications their examination centre is offering.

For students in England, eligibility for the November GCSE English Language and Mathematics series also depends on age and qualification rules. Students should check with their examination centre rather than assuming they can enter every November paper.

November 2026 GCSE resit exam dates

For several major examination boards in England, the November 2026 series begins in the first week of November. Exact papers and dates depend on the awarding body, qualification and tier.

Common GCSE English Language and Maths dates

The following dates apply to common November 2026 GCSE English Language and Mathematics specifications. They should be used as a guide rather than a replacement for your individual examination timetable.

  • Tuesday 3 November: GCSE English Language Paper 1 for several exam boards.
  • Wednesday 4 November: GCSE Mathematics Paper 1 for several exam boards.
  • Thursday 5 November: GCSE English Language Paper 2 for several exam boards.
  • Friday 6 November: GCSE Mathematics Paper 2 for several exam boards.
  • Monday 9 November: GCSE Mathematics Paper 3 for specifications that have three written papers.

Not every board uses the same paper structure. For example, OCR GCSE Mathematics has three papers at each tier, while Pearson Edexcel also includes three papers in its standard GCSE Maths specification.

For students taking WJEC qualifications in Wales, the November arrangements include different dates and specifications. WJEC legacy English Language and Mathematics resit papers are included, while the legacy Mathematics–Numeracy sitting has been moved to January 2027.

Check the official JCQ timetables, WJEC examination timetable, or your awarding body's examination information before planning your final revision sessions.

Important: Always confirm your exam board, qualification code, tier, paper dates and examination arrangements with your school, college or exam centre. November resit schedules are not identical across all UK qualifications.

Yes, November examination arrangements exist across several major UK awarding bodies, but the subjects and qualifications offered differ.

Students may be preparing for GCSE English Language or Mathematics with AQA, Pearson Edexcel, OCR, Eduqas or WJEC, among others. The important point is that revision should match the exact specification being examined.

Is October too late to start tutoring for November GCSE resits?

Not necessarily. Although the examination is close, focused preparation can still be useful if you know what you need to work on.

The most effective approach depends on your starting point.

A student who narrowly missed a target grade in the summer may only need help improving exam technique or correcting a few recurring mistakes. Another student may have larger gaps in subject knowledge and need a more structured approach.

With limited time available, it is important to be realistic. A few tutoring sessions cannot replace months of learning, and no tutor can promise a specific result. However, targeted guidance can help students use their remaining revision time more effectively.

Last-minute GCSE Maths resit revision: what should you prioritise?

GCSE Maths is a subject where gaps in understanding can make several topics feel difficult at once. A student who struggles with fractions, algebra or negative numbers may find many examination questions challenging, even when they understand the general idea.

Instead of trying to revise every topic equally, begin with the areas where you consistently lose marks.

Common revision priorities may include:

  • Number skills: fractions, decimals, percentages, ratios and negative numbers.
  • Algebra: solving equations, rearranging formulas, expanding brackets and factorising.
  • Geometry: angles, area, volume, transformations and Pythagoras' theorem.
  • Statistics and probability: interpreting data, calculating averages and working with probabilities.
  • Problem-solving: understanding unfamiliar questions and choosing appropriate mathematical methods.
  • Exam technique: showing working clearly, checking answers and managing time.

These are examples rather than a complete specification. Students should always use the topic requirements for their own exam board and tier.

For Foundation-tier students, securing reliable methods across core topics can be especially important. Higher-tier students may need to concentrate on multi-step reasoning, algebraic accuracy and more demanding applications.

Last-minute GCSE English Language revision: where should you focus?

English Language revision requires a different approach from Maths. Rather than learning a long list of formulas or methods, students need to practise reading, interpreting and writing under examination conditions.

Common areas where students benefit from focused preparation include:

  • Reading comprehension: identifying information and explaining ideas clearly.
  • Language analysis: discussing the effect of a writer's language choices.
  • Answer structure: organising responses so that they address the question directly.
  • Creative writing: developing descriptions or narratives where required by the specification.
  • Transactional writing: using an appropriate tone, audience and purpose.
  • Accuracy: checking spelling, punctuation, grammar and expression.
  • Timing: completing each section without running out of time.

The exact balance of reading and writing skills depends on the examination board. It is therefore important to practise the correct paper format rather than relying only on general English exercises.

For students sitting English Language in November, feedback on written answers can be particularly valuable. A tutor can help identify why an answer is not reaching the next mark band and demonstrate how it could be improved.

A practical revision plan for the final weeks before November resits

A useful last-minute revision plan should be realistic and focused on the topics that matter most for your examination.

First: Identify where you are losing marks

Start with a recent paper, mock examination or set of past-paper questions. Look at which questions went wrong and why.

Were the mistakes caused by missing knowledge, misunderstanding a question, poor timing or simple errors?

Use the answers to identify your highest-priority topics or skills.

Next: Practise with feedback

Work through targeted examination questions from the correct specification. Review each answer rather than simply moving on to the next question.

For Maths, make sure you understand where a method went wrong and how to correct it. For English Language, focus on how the quality and structure of your answers can improve.

Finally: Focus on exam technique and timing

As the examination approaches, spend more time completing questions under realistic conditions.

Practise allocating time to each section, showing clear working in Maths and planning written responses in English.

The final days should be spent consolidating what you have already practised, rather than trying to learn large amounts of unfamiliar content.
